# Regulation: who rules what, what it costs, and what changed from 2021 to 2026

Last verified: **2026-10-19**. **Not legal advice.** Capital figures and rules change by circular; always read the live regulator page before acting. Where two credible sources disagree we show both.

## Why regulation is the main competitive variable

In Nigeria the regulator is not a referee standing outside the game. A single CBN circular has frozen onboarding at the country's biggest wallets ([S088]), changed who pays a transfer tax ([S092]), and redrawn where a POS agent may stand ([S115]). Capital floors decide who can hold deposits at all ([S082]). If you want to understand who wins, read the circulars first and the pitch decks second.

### Recapitalisation outcomes

* **Banks.** 33 of 37 banks met the new thresholds by the deadline, raising ₦4.65 trillion; the CBN did not name the other four ([S083]).
* **Insurers.** NAICOM reported 50 insurers and reinsurers met the new levels, raising ₦1.079 trillion ([S122]).

## Key rules and events

### Cash and the cashless push

* **Naira redesign, late 2022 to early 2023.** New notes were scarce, POS agents charged 10% to over 20% for cash ([S167]), and e-payment value dipped in February 2023 as failed transfers mounted ([S166]).
* **Cash policy from 1 January 2026.** The CBN removed cash deposit limits and fees, set weekly withdrawal limits of ₦500,000 for individuals and ₦5 million for companies, and charges 3% and 5% on withdrawals above those limits ([S117]).

### Identity, KYC tiers and linkage

* Tier 1 accounts need BVN or NIN; Tier 2 and 3 need both. Funded accounts without them were placed on "Post No Debit or Credit" from 1 March 2024 ([S118]).
* The NCC's final NIN-SIM linkage deadline was 14 September 2024; unlinked lines were barred ([S119]).

### The 2024 onboarding pause

In April 2024 the CBN told OPay, PalmPay, Kuda, Moniepoint and Paga to stop onboarding new customers while it reviewed KYC and account controls. It cleared them to resume in early June 2024, with new customers needing BVN, NIN and proof of address, and address verification for Tier 3 ([S088], [S089]). Several weeks without new signups is a real cost for businesses that run on growth.

### Levies and taxes

| Item | What happened | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Cybersecurity levy | CBN circular of 6 May 2024 asked banks to collect 0.5% on electronic transactions; government suspended it on 14 May 2024 and the CBN withdrew the circular on 17 May | [S090], [S091] |
| Electronic Money Transfer Levy (EMTL) | ₦50 on receipts of ₦10,000 or more; wallets began deducting it from December 2024 | [S093] |
| Stamp duty under the 2025 tax reform | From 1 January 2026 the ₦50 charge is stamp duty paid by the **sender**; transfers under ₦10,000, salaries and own-account transfers are exempt | [S092] |
| Small company exemption | Companies with turnover up to ₦100 million and fixed assets up to ₦250 million are exempt from company income tax and the 4% development levy | [S123] |
| Development levy | 4% of assessable profits for other companies | [S123] |
| Windfall levy on banks' FX gains | 70% on realised FX gains, applied 2023 to 2025 | [S125] |
| FX gains and dividends | CBN barred banks from paying dividends or opex from FX revaluation gains | [S124] |

### FX unification and devaluation

Nigeria unified its FX windows on 16 June 2023 and moved toward a market rate ([S162]). The official annual average went from about ₦1,520/$ in 2025 to about ₦1,385/$ in 2026 on World Bank series data ([S163]). For context, Nairametrics noted the naira was around ₦500/$ in early 2023 ([S006]). Every dollar cost in the stack (terminals, cloud, licences paid to global schemes, USD investor capital) was repriced.

### Crypto: ban, reversal, Binance, ISA 2025

| Date | Event | Source |
|---|---|---|

| 22 Dec 2023 | CBN guidelines let banks open accounts for SEC-licensed VASPs; banks still cannot trade crypto for themselves | [S099], [S100] |

| Feb to Oct 2024 | Binance executive Tigran Gambaryan detained; released in October 2024 after charges dropped | [S101] |

| Oct 2025 | Court hearing on substituted service of the FIRS suit; no final judgment reported by then | [S103] |
| 2025 | ISA 2025 gives SEC explicit powers over virtual asset exchanges and VASPs | [S094] |
| 16 Jan 2026 | SEC sets ₦2 billion capital for digital asset exchanges and custodians | [S097], [S098] |

### Data protection: NDPA 2023 and GAID 2025

The NDPA lets the NDPC impose a remedial fee of the greater of ₦10 million or 2% of the prior year's gross revenue for "data controllers of major importance" ([S109]). GAID 2025 defines those tiers ([S110]). Enforcement is real:

* **Fidelity Bank, August 2024:** ₦555.8 million, which the bank disputed ([S107]).
* **Multichoice Nigeria, July 2025:** ₦766.2 million, including for unlawful cross-border transfers ([S108]).

### FCCPC: Meta and digital lending

* **Meta/WhatsApp.** The tribunal upheld the FCCPC's $220 million fine on 25 April 2025, payable within 60 days ([S104], [S105]). By September 2025 neither side had publicly confirmed payment ([S106]).
* **DEON consumer lending regulations 2025.** In force from 21 July 2025; existing lenders had 90 days to register; corporate fines up to the greater of ₦100 million or 1% of the prior year's turnover, and directors can be disqualified for up to five years ([S111]). The FCCPC set a 5 January 2026 compliance deadline ([S112]) and then moved on violators ([S113]).

### Agent banking and POS geofencing (October 2025)

* Customer cash-out cap ₦100,000 a day and ₦500,000 a week ([S114]).
* Agent cash-out cap ₦1.2 million a day ([S115]).
* Terminals geo-fenced to the registered location; agent location and exclusivity rules from 1 April 2026 ([S115]).
* ₦5 million minimum penalty for geo-tagging breaches ([S116]).

### Other notable rules

* **USSD end user billing.** ₦6.98 per banking USSD transaction of up to 120 seconds, billed to airtime; determination effective March 2025, migration from June 2025 ([S027], [S028]).
* **IMTO guidelines 2024.** Inbound payouts in naira only; cash payouts under $200 equivalent ([S086], [S087]).
* **ISO 20022.** Payment firms had to comply by 31 October 2025 ([S008]).
* **Deposit insurance.** From May 2024, ₦5 million per depositor for banks and MMO subscribers; ₦2 million for MFBs and PSBs ([S120]).
* **Open banking.** Framework and 2023 guidelines exist; not live as of October 2025 ([S024], [S025]).

## Compliance cost and burden

Hard, published numbers on compliance spending are rare. What we can say with sources:

| Cost | Size | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Capital locked to hold a licence | ₦2bn for a switch or MMO, ₦5bn for a PSB or national MFB, ₦50bn to ₦500bn for a bank | [S079], [S081], [S082], [S084] |
| Fines for data breaches | Greater of ₦10m or 2% of revenue | [S109] |
| Fines for digital lending breaches | Greater of ₦100m or 1% of turnover | [S111] |
| POS geo-tagging breaches | ₦5m minimum | [S116] |
| Lost growth during supervisory actions | Onboarding pause from April to early June 2024 | [S088] |
| Physical presence | National MFBs must keep service centres in key locations | [S084] |
| Identity checks | NIMC pricing negotiated by access level; vendors from about $0.04 per check | [S020], [S021] |

What this means in practice:

* **Small firms die on compliance before they die on product.** Thepeer said compliance struggles were part of why it shut down in 2024 ([S176]).
* **Big firms turn compliance into a moat.** Each new rule (geofencing, national licences, ₦2bn VASP capital) raises the cost of entry more than the cost of staying in.
* **Policy risk is two-sided.** The cybersecurity levy lasted about eleven days ([S090], [S091]). Builders should design for rules that change quickly in both directions.
